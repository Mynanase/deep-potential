# Static gravitational potential recovery from 6D phase-space snapshots with Deep Potential, flows, and CBE constraints

This domain studies how to recover a galaxy’s gravitational potential directly from a 6D stellar phase-space snapshot with minimal dynamical assumptions. The field anchor is Green et al. (2022), which introduced Deep Potential: fit a differentiable phase-space distribution with normalizing flows and solve the collisionless Boltzmann equation (CBE) for a neural potential that makes the distribution stationary. For this briefing, the foundation paper and the best reference paper are both arXiv:2011.04673: it is the citer-neighborhood anchor used to define the field and also the most concise methodological entry point before adding later work on mock realism, selection effects, score estimation, non-stationarity diagnostics, and uncertainty-aware validation.

## Methodology

- Core pipeline: learn a smooth differentiable phase-space DF from 6D samples with a normalizing flow, then fit a neural gravitational potential so the learned DF satisfies the stationary CBE.
  Papers: arXiv:2011.04673, arXiv:2305.13358, arXiv:2507.03742
- The DF-gradient-to-acceleration inversion is locally identifiable under equilibrium conditions; in practice one can solve an overdetermined linear system from DF derivatives at shared positions.
  Papers: arXiv:2106.05981, arXiv:2112.07657
- Real progress in this field comes from controlled mock tests with truth access, not just astrophysical plausibility. Validation targets include accelerations, density, dark-matter profile summaries, and failure modes under observational effects.
  Papers: arXiv:2011.04673, arXiv:2205.01129, arXiv:2305.13358, arXiv:2512.02115
- Selection effects are a first-order issue. Two established strategies are joint nuisance learning of selection/dust fields and reformulating the problem around conditional velocity distributions that are invariant to purely spatial selection.
  Papers: arXiv:2412.14236, arXiv:2512.02115, arXiv:2512.09989
- Non-stationarity is not just a caveat but a measurable diagnostic target. Residual asymmetries, phase-space spirals, north-south differences, and tension with independent acceleration probes can be used to decide when static recovery is trustworthy.
  Papers: arXiv:2112.07657, arXiv:2305.13358, arXiv:2501.04075, arXiv:2009.04495, arXiv:2512.09989
- Later method variants suggest that better derivative estimation may matter as much as better density estimation: score matching can bypass noisy explicit density derivatives, while continuous/equivariant flows and entropy regularization offer alternative inductive biases.
  Papers: inspire:3089976, arXiv:2505.00763, arXiv:2406.00236, arXiv:1810.01367, arXiv:1705.07057, arXiv:1908.09257
- Mock-catalog realism and tracer-generation quality matter upstream of inference. Flow-based upsampling for Auriga-like mocks is already part of the local methodological ecosystem and is relevant for truth-controlled experiments in this repository.
  Papers: arXiv:2211.11765

## Known Solved Cases

Use these solved cases as examples of strong research form, not as new ideas. Do not propose a solved case itself as the core deliverable unless the proposal adds a genuinely new scientific component with substantial impact.

- Static-potential recovery from ideal stationary mock snapshots using a learned differentiable DF and CBE stationarity.
  Why solved: The paper demonstrates accurate recovery on ideal mock data and already frames selection effects and non-stationarity as the main next limitations, not the basic static recovery calculation.
  Transferable form: Use a differentiable DF surrogate plus a physics residual tied to a concrete observable target, then validate against simulation truth over a controlled analysis volume.
  Forbidden reuse: Do not propose as new work the same central calculation: train a flow on an equilibrium mock 6D snapshot and infer a static potential by minimizing CBE residuals, with only routine architecture or hyperparameter changes.
  Valid new axes: new observable beyond the original acceleration/density targets, new regime where stationarity is only approximate but diagnostically quantified, new uncertainty-calibration method, new selection-function treatment, new theorem or identifiability result
  Papers: arXiv:2011.04673
- Local acceleration recovery from DF gradients using the CBE, including the practical inversion from learned phase-space distributions and mock validation in the solar neighborhood.
  Why solved: These papers establish both the identifiability logic and a concrete local mock pipeline with strong accuracy in controlled equilibrium settings and explicit disequilibrium diagnostics.
  Transferable form: Pick a local observable with identifiable DF derivatives, derive the inversion clearly, and validate in a region where assumptions and systematics can be stress-tested directly.
  Forbidden reuse: Do not repackage local acceleration recovery from a learned DF in a solar-neighborhood mock, or merely repeat comparisons against Jeans/DF baselines, as a new proposal.
  Valid new axes: new diagnostic for when the local inversion is biased, new estimator for DF derivatives or scores, new tracer-selection strategy, new finite-sample error theory
  Papers: arXiv:2112.07657, arXiv:2106.05981
- Data-driven recovery of acceleration and mass-density fields from realistic simulated or Gaia DR3 stellar samples using flow-estimated DF derivatives and equilibrium assumptions.
  Why solved: These papers already show that the pipeline can recover acceleration and density in realistic settings and expose the current precision limits and equilibrium tensions.
  Transferable form: Target a concrete astrophysical field quantity, work in a bounded volume with sufficient tracer density, and benchmark against simulation truth or independent observational constraints.
  Forbidden reuse: Do not propose as new work simply measuring local Milky Way acceleration or density from Gaia-like data with a flow and CBE, nor merely extending known radial/density profiles with the same logic.
  Valid new axes: different tracer population with materially different systematics, new disequilibrium test tied to the inferred field, new robustness method for second derivatives, new validation template against mock truth
  Papers: arXiv:2205.01129, arXiv:2305.13358
- Extension of Deep Potential to rotating frames, including joint inference of gravitational potential and pattern speed in barred or local Milky Way applications.
  Why solved: The rotating-frame formulation and its simulation/data demonstrations already exist; repeating it would not satisfy the user’s static-potential-first objective.
  Transferable form: Augment the stationarity condition to a modified dynamical frame, then validate against simulations or constrained local data where the extra parameter is observable.
  Forbidden reuse: Do not present rotating-frame Deep Potential or pattern-speed recovery as the main novelty for this task; that line is already established and is outside the user’s primary goal.
  Valid new axes: use rotating-frame ideas only as a nuisance-robustness check for static recovery, derive a non-stationarity veto rather than a main pattern-speed estimator, compare static vs rotating residual structure
  Papers: arXiv:2310.00040, arXiv:2507.03742
- Selection-aware Deep Potential variants: explicit learned dust efficiency and the conditional-velocity formulation that avoids direct spatial selection-function modeling.
  Why solved: These papers already establish both explicit and selection-free routes for handling spatial incompleteness, with mock or data-facing demonstrations and clear claimed advantages.
  Transferable form: Exploit factorization or invariance that removes or simplifies nuisance selection structure, then validate on mocks with known complex selection truth.
  Forbidden reuse: Do not pitch dust/selection correction by jointly learning a highly structured spatial selection map with the potential as the sole novelty, and do not merely restate the conditional-velocity variant without a new scientific component.
  Valid new axes: apply the conditional idea to Auriga-specific mock censoring, compare explicit-selection vs conditional recovery under controlled mismatch, new uncertainty propagation for unknown selection, new identifiability conditions
  Papers: arXiv:2412.14236, arXiv:2512.02115, arXiv:2512.09989, inspire:3089973
- Direct estimation of score fields for CBE-based dark-matter inference without explicit normalizing-flow density evaluation.
  Why solved: The methodological claim that direct score estimation can lower overhead and avoid derivative noise is already articulated and demonstrated on simulated spherical systems.
  Transferable form: Replace a numerically noisy intermediate object with a directly estimated differential quantity when the governing equation depends only on derivatives.
  Forbidden reuse: Do not propose score estimation as new merely because it replaces explicit flow-density derivatives; that substitution itself is already the core contribution of the score-matching paper.
  Valid new axes: JAX-specific estimator/stability comparison in the Deep Potential repository, new score-consistency diagnostic for static-potential recovery, hybrid score-plus-flow scheme with clear scientific payoff
  Papers: inspire:3089976
